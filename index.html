AI Hiring Bias Detector

I reviewed a stack of engineering job descriptions at work and found them loaded with gendered phrasing and age assumptions — "rockstar ninja," "digital native," fifteen years of experience with a three-year-old framework. Nobody writes that on purpose. They copy a template, and qualified people quietly don't apply. Flags are matched against a curated lexicon rather than generated freehand, so the same posting scores the same way twice.

02

EU AI Act Compliance Checker

I spent too many hours reading EU AI Act PDFs while reviewing AI features, looking up the same article numbers repeatedly. Models get article numbers wrong confidently, so this one doesn't ask it to remember them — the Act is chunked and embedded, the relevant articles are retrieved first, and any citation the model produces that isn't in the retrieved set gets dropped before it reaches you.

03

LLM Hallucination Tester

Built because I was tired of models stating nonsense with total confidence. Three stages: generate verifiable questions, answer them, then grade each answer. The grading is the part that matters — instead of asking a second model whether the first one was right, each answer is checked against retrieved reference passages, and the supporting passage comes back with the verdict so you can see what the judgement rested on.

    How these demos handle your data
    What is sent
    Only the text you type into a demo box, capped at 2,000 characters. Nothing else about you or your browser.
    Where it goes
    A container on Google Cloud Run that wakes on request and sleeps after, which calls the Google Gemini API. Free-tier prompts may be used by Google to improve their models.
    What is retained
    Nothing. Request bodies are never logged — only timestamp, path and status code are recorded.
    Limits
    Five runs per hour per visitor, and outbound model calls are capped at two concurrently. When a limit is reached you'll see a cached sample result rather than an error.
    Uploads
    Not accepted. File parsing exists in the repositories but is deliberately left out of the public demos.
